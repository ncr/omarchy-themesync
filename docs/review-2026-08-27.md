# Independent review of BEACON.md — 2026-08-27

Written by a separate reviewer session (Claude Opus) asked to find wrong or fragile
assumptions in `protocol/BEACON.md`, the Rust host and the watch firmware, and to compare
with established practice. Verbatim, ordered by severity. Decisions taken on it are recorded
in `BEACON.md` itself.

---

## 1. The state beacon is unauthenticated *and* every applied beacon writes flash. "The worst an attacker can do is recolour your watch" is false.

**Assumed** (BEACON.md): "No authentication: the worst an attacker can do is recolour the watch."

**Actual**: `beacon_handle()` (ble.c:172) calls `theme_set_wire()` for every beacon whose `seq` differs from the last applied, and `theme_set_wire()` unconditionally persists (theme.c:225-226): `publish(&t); nvs_save(...)`.

Concrete attack, no key needed: advertise `FF FF 54 01 <seq++> 00 <v2 packet>` at 20 ms with an incrementing seq. Every packet has a new seq, so every packet is applied and committed to NVS. At ~50 Hz you reach ESP-IDF NVS's practical write budget in hours. Cheaper version: set bg = fg = `000000` and hold it — a permanently unreadable watch face that survives reboot (it is loaded from NVS at `theme_init`). Neither requires the pairing key, neither is detectable, and the watch has no way to prefer the desktop it paired with because nothing reads the `host` byte.

Same hole over GATT: `THEME_COLORS_UUID` is `F_READ | F_WRITE` with no encryption/authentication flag (ble.c:444-446), and a GATT theme write also *completes the watch's pending request* (ble.c:277) — so any central within range can write themes and forge request completions.

**Prior art**: BLE Mesh has no unauthenticated broadcast tier — every network PDU carries a NetMIC (32/64-bit). iBeacon/Eddystone are unauthenticated precisely because a receiver *does nothing but display* what it hears, with no persistence. Eddystone-EID exists because even a beacon identifier needs authentication once anything acts on it. This receiver commits to flash.

**Recommendation**
- Split "apply" from "persist". Persist only after the theme has been stable ~10 s, and only when the bytes differ from what is stored. Removes the flash-wear vector regardless of everything else.
- Add a 4-byte HMAC to the beacon over the whole packet body. The watch already holds the key and computes HMAC-SHA256 for requests and list COMMITs. Check it after the content-differs test, so "same theme again" costs nothing. This makes the `host` byte redundant: the key *is* the desktop identity.
- Until the MAC exists, at minimum latch the `host` byte at pairing and ignore beacons from other hosts.

## 2. Lost requests are a bug in the daemon's read path, not a property of BLE. The debounce is a workaround for it.

**Assumed** (README, adv.rs): requests get lost because a short-lived advertisement can be missed by a coalescing scanner, so the watch must keep each request on the air long enough.

**Actual**: bluer's `discover_devices_with_changes()` re-emits `DeviceAdded(addr)` on *any* device property change — the event carries only the address. `scan_ours` then does a **fresh D-Bus round trip** to read the current property (adv.rs:75-76). So the daemon does not see a stream of advertisements; it sees a stream of *wakeups* and samples whatever is in BlueZ's cache when its round trip completes. If request A is replaced by B before that round trip lands, A is never observed — even though BlueZ received it, cached it, and signalled it.

Worse, this happens once per advertisement *of every BLE device in range*, because `DuplicateData: true` makes BlueZ emit a property change for each duplicate report — hundreds of D-Bus round trips per second into a single tokio task.

**Fix**: `DeviceEvent::PropertyChanged(DeviceProperty::ManufacturerData(map))` carries the value from the signal. Subscribe with `dev.events()` and decode the value out of the event: no round trip, no sampling. Also cheaply ignore devices that have never carried company `0xFFFF`.

**Prior art**: "signal-and-read vs value-in-the-event" — inotify carries the event; kqueue's EVFILT_VNODE forces a re-stat and loses intermediate states; netlink route messages carry the changed attributes rather than a "something changed" ping.

**On the 300 ms debounce, drop the minimum on-air time**: sound, do it for UI reasons. But it is a mitigation, not the fix. Dropping the minimum on-air time is correct: SET is absolute, the newest request replaces the old one on air, the desktop coalesces to the newest pending SET.

## 3. `seq` is the wrong primitive. The beacon already *is* reported state — compare content, and the nonce/ack dance disappears.

**Assumed**: receivers re-apply when `seq` differs from the last one they applied; the `0x42` ack record is needed to tell "this answers my request" from "the desktop changed on its own" and from "this answers an older request".

Three concrete failures:

*(a) A dropped beacon marks its seq as consumed and strands the watch permanently.* `theme_set_wire()` returns `true` even when it **ignores** the packet (theme.c:221-224, the `expect` check), and the caller records the seq as applied (ble.c:172-174). Scenario: watch swipes to `nord`, `expect = "nord"`; before the desktop answers, the user changes the desktop by hand to `gruvbox` → beacon seq 42, slug `gruvbox`. The watch drops it *and records 42 as applied*. The request times out, `expect` clears — but the desktop keeps broadcasting seq 42 forever. Watch shows `nord`, desktop `gruvbox`, nothing converges until the next desktop change.

*(b) Desktop restart, 1-in-256.* `seq` is seeded from `epoch_secs & 0xff`. If the seed equals the watch's last applied seq, the watch ignores the desktop until the next change. Same after a 256-change wrap.

*(c) Two desktops.* Nothing reads the `host` byte on the watch. Two beaconing desktops make every packet "new"; the watch repaints — and writes NVS — at up to 10 Hz.

**Prior art**: MQTT retained messages and desired/reported state (AWS Shadow, Kubernetes spec/status) carry the *value*, not a change counter, so a late joiner, a restarted publisher and a duplicate delivery all converge without sequence state. CoAP Observe carries a sequence number only for reordering inside a short window and RFC 7641 falls back to the payload beyond it. RFC 1982 serial arithmetic exists because "differs from last" and "greater than last" are both broken across restarts unless wrap is defined — the simplest escape is not to have a serial.

**Recommendation**
1. Replace "seq differs" with "the theme payload differs from what I am showing": `s_applied_crc = crc16(theme bytes, excluding the ack record)`. `seq` becomes a debug field or goes away.
2. Delete the `0x42` ack record and the nonce's ack role. A SET is satisfied when the beacon reports the slug you asked for — exactly what `theme_expect()` already implements. RESEND needs no ack (receiving a beacon is the answer). LIST is completed by the COMMIT. "Two quick swipes" is handled by `expect`: while SET(Y) is outstanding ignore any beacon whose slug isn't Y; on timeout clear `expect` and `s_applied_crc` and let the next beacon win.
3. Fix `theme_set_wire()` to return a tri-state (APPLIED / IGNORED / BAD) so "ignored" can never be recorded as "applied".

Deletes: `seq`, the epoch seed, wrap handling, the `0x42` record, `AckLog` (~30 lines + 30 s TTL + consume-once), `s_recent_nonces`, the nonce's dual role. Costs 2 bytes of watch RAM.

## 4. The counter: right instinct, wrong scope as sketched, and one silent-failure mode you must design for.

The **correctness** justification is stronger than the security one. Today's dedup is `(address, nonce, op, arg) != last seen from that address`, never expiring, plus a start-up sweep that marks everything in BlueZ's cache as seen. That has a bug you hit every deploy: **`systemctl --user restart themesync` while a request is on the air silently kills that request** — the cached sweep marks it seen, the press does nothing for its whole 10 s life.

A persisted counter with "accept iff `ctr > last`" deletes `seen`, the `cached` flag, the start-up sweep, and the daemon-restart bug. That is the argument to make; the replay argument alone is over-engineering.

Details:
- **Scope is the key, not the address.** The MAC covers bytes 0..6 and does not bind the source address, so a replayed packet from a *spoofed* address bypasses per-address dedup. Worse, `watch_addr` is set from the request's source address and is what LIST pushes connect to — a spoofed LIST makes the daemon open an outbound GATT connection to an attacker-chosen address. Key the counter by key; do not let an unverified address redirect `watch_addr`. Prior art: Wi-Fi 4-way handshake MICs cover nonces *and* MAC addresses for this reason.
- **`u16` is enough**, but do not persist on every increment. Prior art: **BLE Mesh sequence-number block allocation** — persist `ctr + 100` at boot, hand out counters up to that, persist the next block when exhausted. A crash costs ≤100 counters, tolerated by strictly-greater. Zigbee frame counters, same convention.
- **Strictly greater, no window, one counter per key, not per op.** BLE Mesh replay-protection list and Zigbee: per source, single counter, strictly greater.
- **Desync is silent; make it loud.** Reflash (NVS erased) → counter 0 while the desktop says 4711 → every request rejected forever with no symptom beyond "no answer". BLE Mesh needed a whole IV Index Recovery procedure for this. Minimum: log `request ctr 3 <= last accepted 4711 — re-pair, or run themesync reset-counter`, surface in `themesync status`, document that re-pairing resets both. Do not auto-accept a large backward jump.
- **Ack byte: the full counter or nothing** (better nothing, per finding 3).
- Mechanics: body 6 → 7 bytes, `REQUEST_LEN` 10 → 11, HMAC over bytes 0..7, AD budget 28 ≤ 31. Bump `kind` to `0x03` so a mixed-version pair produces "unknown kind" rather than "bad length".
- **Write the counter file atomically** (temp + rename); `save_key` uses a bare `fs::write`.

## 5. The desktop's scan is **active**, not passive. The spec says the opposite.

BlueZ's `SetDiscoveryFilter` has no passive/active option; the kernel uses `LE_SCAN_ACTIVE` for discovery (that is how it harvests scan-response names — how `transport/ble.rs` finds the watch by the UUID in its scan response). So the desktop sends a SCAN_REQ to the watch on essentially every advertising event, forever; the watch answers with a SCAN_RSP each time (`p.scannable = 1`). An extra TX every 200 ms on the battery device, caused by the mains-powered one. BlueZ also holds a `Discovering` session forever (~100 % scan duty, interval == window) while advertising and carrying A2DP.

**Recommendation**: fix the spec text; consider making the watch's advertisement non-scannable while a request is on the air (the service UUID only matters when a desktop wants to connect).

## 6. Scan-window arithmetic: 120 ms does **not** reliably catch the 100 ms steady beacon.

adv.rs asks for `min_interval = 100 ms, max_interval = 110 ms`. The controller may pick anywhere in that range. Worst case: 110 ms + advDelay 0–10 ms (Core Vol 6 Part B, mandatory pseudo-random advDelay on every advertising event) = **up to 120 ms between events on a given primary channel**. The watch's window is exactly 120 ms, `itvl == window` on the slow scan, so it sits on one primary channel for the whole window. Window == max gap, zero margin — before accounting for extended advertising: the primary channel carries only ADV_EXT_IND with an AuxPtr; the scanner must follow to a secondary channel for the ~0.8 ms AUX_ADV_IND. An ADV_EXT_IND in the last moments of the window may not get its aux chased; one lost AUX loses the whole event.

Bites only in steady state — which is exactly the resync path: screen wake, daemon restart, a missed change, the post-timeout repaint.

**Recommendation**: `max_interval == min_interval` and drop steady to 80 ms (worst gap 90 ms vs 120 ms window = 30 ms margin); or keep 100 ms and raise the window to 200 ms (6.7 % duty vs 4.7 %). Also: `BEACON_PERIOD_UNITS 2` = 2.56 s while the spec says 3 s — pick one. Nothing verifies the interval BlueZ actually programmed; measure once with `btmon` and record it.

## 7. Pairing: the key travels in clear, and any stranger can pop the pairing screen.

`THEME_KEY_UUID` is `F_WRITE` only — no `F_WRITE_ENC` / `F_WRITE_AUTHEN`, nothing requires LE Secure Connections. So: the 16-byte key is sniffable during the pairing window; once lifted, requests can be forged forever (the counter does not help). Any central can write `[0x01][code][key]` and **open the Pairing screen on the user's watch** — unauthenticated, unrate-limited UI hijack; if the user dials the attacker's digits (1/256 blind, 100 % if told), the attacker owns the watch. `pair_key_load()` falls back to a **hard-coded key in the firmware source** and sets `s_paired = true` for it.

**Prior art**: LE Secure Connections numeric comparison (ECDH + confirmation over the transcript); Signal safety numbers; Noise short-authenticated-string. Key *transport* plus a confirmation code protects against pairing the wrong device; it does not protect the key.

**Recommendation**, increasing effort: (1) accept a pending key only within ~60 s of the user opening "Pair" on the watch; (2) `BLE_GATT_CHR_F_WRITE_ENC` on the key characteristic (desktop must trigger LE pairing first); (3) X25519 over `…0005`, key = HKDF(shared secret), the digits a SAS over the transcript hash, widened to 16 bits; (4) default key behind a build flag, off by default, `ble_is_paired()` false while in use.

## 8. Addressing themes by `crc16(slug)` is silently lossy and does double duty as a staleness check.

With ~22 themes the birthday probability of a collision is ≈ 0.4 %, and the daemon takes the *first* match with no collision check. For an affected user that is a permanent, silent "tapping A gives B". "crc matches nothing" conflates a stale list with a corrupt argument and a collision, and detects staleness only for *removed* themes.

**Recommendation**: `arg = [list index u8][list crc8 u8]`, crc8 over the list bytes the watch holds. Mismatch → "your list is stale, here it is" (deterministic, catches renames and additions); match → index, no collisions. This is HTTP `If-Match`/ETag (CoAP ETag) applied where it belongs. At minimum, detect collisions in `themelist::build()`.

## 9. The theme-list COMMIT MAC signs the payload, not the transfer — so it replays.

Nothing binds the MAC to *this* transfer. Anyone who observed a push can connect to `…0006` (unauthenticated) and replay BEGIN/DATA/COMMIT later, rolling the picker back. Self-heals via the unknown-slug path, so low severity — but the fix is free: `mac = HMAC(key, 0x03 || total_le || crc_le || list_bytes)[0..4]`, and append the request counter once it exists. Prior art: TLS `Finished` over the handshake hash; Noise chains `h`.

## 10. Smaller, but real

- `host` crc8 is dead weight: nothing reads it. Wire it up (latch at pairing) or delete it; a beacon MAC subsumes it.
- A failed beacon re-registration leaves you dark for up to 5 minutes (`set_beacon` drops the old handle and registers a new one with no await on the unregister; on error only logs; next repair is the 300 s tick). Retry once after ~200 ms; shorten the republish interval.
- Nonce collision under the current scheme ~0.4 % per press: `req_emit` guarantees only `nonce != s_last_nonce`, while the desktop's `seen` holds the last *accepted* tuple. The counter removes this.
- The post-timeout repaint should live in `req_timer_cb` only, not in `req_finish` (which also runs on success, where forgetting the applied state causes a redundant re-apply and NVS write).
- Spec/code drift: `theme_expect()` appears nowhere in BEACON.md. BEACON.md says the watch "expects the answer within a second" while `omarchy-theme-set` takes 2–5 s. README says LIST is op `0x06`; it is `0x03`. Watch period 2.56 s vs spec 3 s.

## Assumptions that are actually fine

- **4-byte HMAC truncation.** Online forgery ~2³² delivered advertisements; truncation leaks nothing about the key. BLE Mesh's 32-bit NetMIC is the same trade-off. Keep 4 bytes; log/rate-limit failures so brute force is visible.
- **ATT application error codes 0x80–0x85.** Legal (0x80–0x9F reserved for application errors), correctly decoded, correctly non-retryable.
- **MTU handling.** `frame_len_for_mtu` clamps to `[20, 509]`; 509 ≤ 512−3 matches the watch's buffer; the 23-MTU floor degrades to 73 slow writes. Strictly sequential, no resume, fresh BEGIN on retry — correct for 1.4 KB.
- **Watch address stability.** `ble_hs_id_infer_auto(0, …)` with privacy off yields the public address, so dedup-by-address works *today*. Write that into the spec as a requirement; a key-scoped counter removes the dependency.
- **AD budget.** 3 + 10 + 14 = 27 ≤ 31; still fits at 28 with an 11-byte request.
- **Legacy PDU on the ext-adv API** — correct and necessary; the configure-once fix for `BLE_HS_EBUSY` is right.
- **`filter_duplicates = 2` on the slow scan** — reset-per-period is right; no filter on the dense scan is fine.
- **SET to the already-active theme still produces an ack** — `omarchy-theme-set` runs the hook unconditionally.
- **Concurrent theme changes don't corrupt state** — `omarchy-theme-set` takes an flock; the lock is released before the hook runs, so the actor's newest-wins coalescing is still needed and correct.
- **No deadlock in the hook path** — the hook re-execs `sync --async` detached.
- **Optimistic local paint + reconciliation** is the right pattern; `theme_expect` is the right filter. The complaint in finding 3 is the *second* mechanism next to it.
- **The 30 ms / 10 s burst** is well sized: ~4 events per 120 ms window.
- **Extended advertising at ~90 bytes** fits a single AUX_ADV_IND; a 4-byte MAC costs nothing here.

## Verdict

The design is sound in its bones: connection-less broadcast for a small, idempotent, frequently-repeated state; a keyed, absolute request channel; big and rare things over GATT. Those are the right three choices.

What is wrong is that the state machine has accumulated a second mechanism for every job — `seq` *and* content, the ack nonce *and* `theme_expect`, the address dedup *and* the cached-sweep heuristic — and each pair has a gap the other doesn't cover. The counter is the right move but aimed at the third-most-important problem, and being added on top of the redundancy rather than used to remove it. Meanwhile the direction that actually persists to flash is the unauthenticated one.

Top 3, in order:

1. **Stop persisting on every applied beacon, and make the apply rule content-based.** Debounce the NVS write, fix `theme_set_wire()`'s return value, replace "seq differs" with "payload differs", delete the `0x42` ack in favour of `theme_expect`. Closes the flash-wear DoS, the seq-wrap and restart-reseed holes, the stranded-watch bug; removes `AckLog`, `s_recent_nonces`, and the nonce's ack role.
2. **Read the manufacturer data out of the D-Bus signal** instead of re-reading the property after a wakeup. The actual cause of lost requests; also removes a D-Bus round trip per advertisement of every device in the room. Keep the 300 ms debounce; drop the minimum-on-air idea.
3. **Land the counter — scoped per key, block-allocated on the watch, desync logged loudly** — and use it to *delete* `seen`, the `cached` flag, the start-up cache sweep, and the address-keyed dedup. Bump `kind` to `0x03`; write both counter files atomically.

Second pass: authenticate the beacon with the 4-byte HMAC (finding 1), gate the pairing write behind a user action on the watch (finding 7), switch theme addressing to `[index][list crc8]` (finding 8).
